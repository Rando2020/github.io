Executive summary

Orivo Health turns patient language into care-team action.

Orivo is an SMS-first patient communication and clinical-context layer. The product helps patients describe medication questions, access barriers, and safety concerns in plain language, then routes structured summaries to the appropriate pharmacy, provider, health plan, or care team.

Mission

Make healthcare understandable.

Reduce the distance between what patients say and what healthcare teams need to know.

Initial wedge

Medication communication.

Start where confusion, access barriers, and adherence risk already collide.

Strategic asset

Patient-reported context.

Create a safer, reviewable data layer from real-world patient language.

2
Problem

The patient side of healthcare is still underbuilt.

Patients do not usually describe their problems in clinical terms. They say they feel dizzy, cannot get a ride, do not know what a medication is for, cannot afford a refill, or are scared to keep taking something. Most systems capture these signals late, if at all.

Medication nonadherence is common and costly, but many interventions still rely on calls, portals, apps, and generic reminders.
Health literacy gaps mean many patients cannot translate medication labels, provider instructions, or benefit details into action.
Access barriers such as transportation, food insecurity, affordability, and care navigation often show up as casual patient comments.
Clinical teams need the original patient voice, not just an AI summary.
Buyers need structured, measurable workflows, not vague chatbot promises.
3
Solution

Aevo listens. Vera validates. Humans decide.

Aevo

Patient communication AI

Receives patient messages through SMS-first workflows.
Asks plain-language clarifying questions when safe and appropriate.
Extracts barrier type, medication concern, urgency tier, and patient intent.
Preserves the original patient message for human review.
Vera

Validation and data intelligence

Checks response boundaries, escalation rules, and claim safety.
Supports de-identified trend analysis for payer and operational reporting.
Flags low-confidence outputs rather than guessing.
Creates a pathway from individual concern to population-level learning.

5
Target customers

Start where the business pain is already budgeted.

Primary beachhead

Health plans

Best fit when Orivo can support medication adherence, HRSN signal capture, member engagement, and care-management routing.

Second beachhead

Pharmacy partners

Best fit when Orivo can increase useful patient context before pharmacist outreach or medication review.

Downstream user

Providers

Providers receive structured context but should not be the first buyer unless a clinic has clear budget and operational urgency.

10
Go-to-market

Sell one workflow, not the whole future.

Step 1

Design partner

Find one health plan, pharmacy group, or clinic with an existing medication barrier workflow.

Step 2

Paid pilot

Run a narrow workflow with agreed success metrics, safety rules, and operational owner.

Step 3

Expand by evidence

Only add modules after proving routing quality, adoption, and measurable operational value.

11
Operating model

Build like healthcare buyers are watching from day one.

Product lead owns scope, claims discipline, pilot metrics, and buyer feedback loops.
Clinical advisor owns escalation policies, unsafe-response boundaries, and review protocols.
Security and compliance owner defines BAAs, access control, audit logging, data retention, incident response, and vendor review.
Engineering starts with secure messaging, intake classification, dashboard views, and audit trails before advanced analytics.
Implementation owns onboarding, workflow mapping, training, support, and pilot retrospectives.
12
Compliance and safety

The safety model is part of the product.

Never claim casually

HIPAA compliance

Use HIPAA-eligible vendors, execute BAAs, and design safeguards before PHI. Do not publicly claim full compliance until controls are implemented and reviewed.

Never allow

AI clinical autonomy

Orivo should not diagnose, prescribe, tell a patient to stop medication, or minimize symptoms. It should educate, flag, route, and escalate.

13
Roadmap

Four phases from prototype to platform.

Phase 0

Prototype and validation

Finalize narrative, clickable demos, safety boundaries, and design-partner materials.

Phase 1

Narrow pilot MVP

SMS intake, classification, Orivo Brief, action queue, consent, audit logging, and human review.

Phase 2

Operational expansion

Integrations, reporting, user roles, admin tools, pharmacy workflows, and payer reporting.

Phase 3

Data intelligence layer

De-identified barrier trends, quality reporting support, segmentation, and productized analytics.

Funding and use of funds

GTM, design partner development, investor materials, and market validation.

Claims boundary: This plan should be used as a strategic and fundraising artifact. It does not prove Orivo-specific outcomes, revenue, compliance, clinical quality, or buyer adoption. Any production use involving PHI requires legal, security, clinical, and operational review.
