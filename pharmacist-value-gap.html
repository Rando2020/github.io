The problem

Provider status is the policy fight. Documentation specificity is the operational fight.

The reimbursement struggle is not simply that pharmacists lack value. Complex medication safety work is frequently reduced to short notes that do not show why the decision mattered, who reviewed it, and what happened for the patient.

Strategic Orivo thesis: as reimbursement models evolve, organizations that can prove pharmacist work with patient-specific reasoning, timestamps, supervision context, care-team routing, and outcomes will be better positioned than teams relying on checkbox documentation.
Product concept

Orivo Rx Ledger

A medication optimization documentation layer for pharmacist-led care. It does not replace clinical judgment. It captures the evidence trail around that judgment.

Patient voice capturePreserves the original SMS or voice concern, including plain-language barriers, side effects, confusion, and goals.
Clinical translationStructures the concern as medication issue, adherence risk, symptom concern, access barrier, benefit question, or escalation candidate.
Reasoning builderPrompts for indication, risk, benefit, alternatives, patient factors, counseling provided, monitoring plan, and outcome.
Provider handoff packetRoutes pharmacist reasoning to the supervising provider, prescriber, or care team for review and action.
Billing-readiness checklistHighlights documentation fields often needed for reimbursement review, including time, supervision path, care plan linkage, patient communication, and duplication risk.
Outcome ledgerTracks what changed: counseling completed, medication clarified, patient escalated, barrier resolved, provider reviewed, follow-up scheduled.

Why now

The market is moving toward team-based medication management.

CMS guidance recognizes supervised medication deprescribing as part of physician fee schedule care management workflows, and ASHP continues to advocate around pharmacist incident-to billing limitations. Orivo can sit beneath that policy movement as the evidence layer.

✓CMS incident-to rules require services to meet specific setting, supervision, and treatment requirements.
✓ASHP notes pharmacists are not currently recognized as Medicare Part B qualified health professionals.
✓Supervised medication deprescribing guidance reinforces the need for care planning, monitoring, communication, and care-team coordination.
✓Orivo is positioned as documentation and care-team support, not direct billing automation.
Claims boundary: Orivo does not claim pharmacists can bill independently today. The product is positioned for future reimbursement readiness: making medication optimization work more specific, reviewable, and auditable so care teams can better demonstrate the value of pharmacist-led interventions as policy and payer models evolve.
