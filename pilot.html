Pilot thesis

Start narrow. Prove the signal.

The first proof is not PDC lift, Star Ratings improvement, or ADE reduction. The first proof is whether patients use a simple SMS channel, whether Orivo captures useful barriers, and whether the care team trusts the routed context.

Pilot model

Four phases. Clear gates. No magical claims.

Weeks 1–2
Setup and safety boundaries
Define population, messages, escalation rules, benefit language, human owners, consent flow, and pilot-safe scope.
Weeks 3–4
Enrollment and activation
Enroll patients through pharmacy, plan outreach, clinic workflow, QR code, or direct SMS invitation.
Weeks 5–10
Live barrier capture
Capture medication confusion, side effects, cost friction, transportation barriers, telehealth demand, and unresolved questions.
Weeks 11–12
Analysis and pilot readout
Deliver barrier analytics, care-team usefulness scoring, intervention logs, and recommendations for scale.

What Orivo delivers

A real pilot package should end with artifacts, not vibes.

Barrier intelligence report

De-identified trends by barrier type, medication concern, ZIP/county where applicable, telehealth demand, and benefit friction.

Care-team routing report

Breakdown of issues routed to pharmacy, provider, care coordinator, payer team, or emergency guidance boundary.

Intervention log

Human-reviewed actions, callbacks, counseling notes, unresolved barriers, and resolution follow-up outcomes.

Patient language library

De-identified examples of how patients describe medication confusion, cost issues, side effects, rides, and appointment barriers.

Workflow recommendations

What should remain SMS, what needs pharmacist review, what belongs to care coordination, and what requires provider triage.

Scale decision memo

Go, no-go, or modify recommendation with risk, compliance, staffing, integration, and buyer-value assessment.

What we need from a partner

Simple pilot inputs. Clear ownership.

A pilot should not require a full enterprise integration on day one. Start with a bounded population, safe workflow, and a human owner for each route.

✓Defined patient/member population and outreach channel
✓Approved message templates and escalation language
✓Named pharmacist, provider, care coordinator, or payer owner
✓Benefit language approved by the plan or partner
✓Privacy, security, consent, and BAA review before production workflows
What Orivo does not claim yet
✕Does not claim validated PDC lift without pilot data
✕Does not claim Star Ratings improvement without payer validation
✕Does not claim ADE reduction without clinical study design
✕Does not diagnose, prescribe, or change medication instructions
✕Does not replace pharmacists, providers, or care teams
What Orivo is trying to prove
✓Patients will text concerns in plain language
✓Useful barriers can be surfaced before claims data catches up
✓Care teams can act faster with structured briefs
✓Plans can learn benefit and access gaps from de-identified patterns
✓Human-in-the-loop AI can be safer and more credible than chatbot-only design
Pilot boundary: Orivo pilots require partner-specific legal, security, consent, HIPAA/BAA, clinical escalation, and workflow review before any real patient data, PHI, or production clinical routing is used.
