The research behind the Orivo pitch.

This page documents public research and official guidance used to support Orivo Health's market problem, product rationale, pharmacist documentation thesis, and technical design direction. Each numbered card matches a citation marker elsewhere on the site.

Context: Orivo is pre-seed and prototype-stage. These sources support the healthcare problem space and product rationale. They do not represent Orivo-specific clinical outcomes, pilot results, reimbursement guarantees, or production deployment claims.
Problem evidence

Medication communication is a measurable healthcare problem.

Orivo's first wedge is based on connected realities: roughly half of patients don't take long-term medications as prescribed, this nonadherence is costly, patients often struggle with health literacy, and many digital tools assume app access or portal behavior.

1
Adherence
Approximately 50% of patients don't take long-term medications as prescribed.
The CDC and WHO both report that adherence to long-term therapies in developed countries averages around 50%. WHO described this in its 2003 adherence report; CDC reaffirmed the figure in MMWR Grand Rounds.
CDC MMWR Grand RoundsWHO Adherence Report
2
Adherence
Medication nonadherence is associated with roughly 125,000 preventable deaths annually in the U.S.
Published estimates commonly cite approximately 125,000 deaths annually in the U.S. associated with medication nonadherence and annual cost estimates in the $100B to $300B range. JAMA has also described nonadherence as common among adults taking long-term medications.
Public Health ReportsJAMA
3
Adverse drug events
Adverse drug events cause roughly $3.5B in excess U.S. medical costs each year.
Each year in the U.S., adverse drug events (ADEs) are linked to approximately 1.5 million emergency department visits, 500,000 hospitalizations, and $3.5B in excess medical costs. Tracked under HHS's Healthy People 2030 Medical Product Safety workgroup.
HHS Healthy People 2030
4
Health literacy
Only 12% of U.S. adults have proficient health literacy.
This supports the need for plain-language healthcare communication and medication education workflows that do not depend on clinical vocabulary.
NCBI BookshelfAHRQ NHQDR
5
Medication therapy
Non-optimized medication therapy represents a large economic burden.
A frequently cited estimate places the annual U.S. cost of non-optimized medication therapy at $528.4B. This is broader than nonadherence alone and includes additional medication-related problems.
NASPA summary of Watanabe et al.
6
SMS-first access
Cellphone access is broad enough to justify a no-app communication wedge.
Pew reported that 98% of U.S. adults own a cellphone of some kind and 91% own a smartphone. Orivo's SMS-first approach is designed to support both basic phones and smartphones.
Pew Research Center Mobile Fact Sheet
Buyer relevance

The wedge connects to pharmacy and payer quality economics.

Orivo's product direction is designed around adherence workflows, PDC-tracked medication classes, pharmacist review, and health-related social needs that can interfere with medication use.

7
PDC
PDC is a standard adherence methodology.
PQA describes Proportion of Days Covered as its preferred method for adherence measures and describes the common 80% threshold for many chronic medication classes.
PQA Adherence Measures
8
Star Ratings
Medication adherence is part of Medicare Part D Star Ratings.
CMS includes Part D adherence measures for diabetes medications, hypertension/RAS antagonists, and statins in Star Ratings reporting.
CMS Star Ratings Fact Sheet
9
HRSN
Transportation, food access, housing, utilities, and financial strain can affect care use.
CMS defines health-related social needs as individual social and economic needs affecting health and health care use, including food, housing, utilities, health care access, and transportation.
CMS SDOH/HRSNMedicaid.gov HRSN
Pharmacist value gap

The pharmacist reimbursement argument needs stronger proof of clinical reasoning.

These sources explain the current reimbursement gap and support Orivo's documentation-specific product position: helping care teams capture the evidence behind pharmacist-led medication optimization while avoiding direct-billing claims.

12
Provider status
ASHP states pharmacists are not currently recognized as Medicare Part B qualified health professionals.
ASHP's incident-to billing guidance says pharmacists may support physician-billed services under certain pathways, but direct recognition under Medicare Part B remains a policy gap.
ASHP Incident-to Billing
13
Incident-to rules
CMS incident-to services have specific treatment, setting, and supervision requirements.
CMS describes incident-to services and supplies under the Physician Fee Schedule. Orivo's product role is documentation and coordination support, not autonomous billing.
CMS Incident-to Services
14
Deprescribing
CMS guidance on supervised medication deprescribing emphasizes care planning, monitoring, communication, coordination, and pharmacist involvement.
The guidance supports Orivo's product thesis that medication optimization work benefits from structured documentation: patient symptoms, care plan linkage, pharmacist involvement, communication, and care-team coordination.
CMS Deprescribing Guidance PDF

Claims boundary

What the sources support, and what they do not.

  • The sources support the need for better medication communication, adherence support, health-literacy-aware education, patient-reported barrier capture, and pharmacist documentation specificity.
  • The sources support Orivo's product rationale, not Orivo-specific outcomes.
  • Current product language describes Orivo as designed to support adherence workflows, pharmacist review, patient-reported concern capture, medication optimization documentation, and care-team routing.
  • Public claims remain limited: Orivo does not claim it improves PDC, reduces adverse drug events, improves Star Ratings, enables direct pharmacist reimbursement, or is HIPAA compliant in production until those claims are validated.
