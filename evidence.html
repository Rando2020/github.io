Evidence-backed claims · Public source register

The claims behind the Orivo pitch.

This page documents the public research and official guidance used to support Orivo Health's market problem, product wedge, and technical roadmap. It also separates supported market claims from Orivo pilot hypotheses.

Important: Orivo is pre-seed. The sources below support the healthcare problem and product rationale. They do not claim Orivo has achieved clinical outcomes, improved adherence, reduced adverse drug events, or completed a HIPAA-compliant production deployment.
Claim matrix

Supported claims that can stay public.

Adherence
Medication nonadherence is common, costly, and linked to preventable harm.
The U.S. Surgeon General published estimates that about 125,000 deaths per year are due to medication nonadherence, with estimated annual cost ranges of $100B to $300B. JAMA also describes nonadherence as common and costly, estimating 30% to 50% of U.S. adults are not adherent to long-term medications.
Surgeon General / Public Health Reports · JAMA
Medication therapy
Non-optimized medication therapy has been estimated at $528.4B annually.
Use this carefully. The $528.4B figure is for non-optimized medication therapy, not only nonadherence. Do not label it as “nonadherence cost” without that nuance.
NASPA summary of Watanabe et al.
Health literacy
Only 12% of U.S. adults have proficient health literacy.
This supports Orivo's plain-language medication communication wedge. Use “health literacy” broadly, not “only 12% understand discharge instructions,” unless separately sourced.
NCBI Bookshelf / Surgeon General Workshop · AHRQ National Healthcare Quality and Disparities Report
Access
SMS-first is strategically plausible because cell phone ownership is nearly universal.
Pew reported that 98% of U.S. adults own a cellphone of some kind and 91% own a smartphone in 2025. This supports “basic-phone capable” and “no app required” positioning.
Pew Research Center Mobile Fact Sheet
PDC
PDC is the preferred method used by PQA adherence measures.
PQA states that Proportion of Days Covered is its preferred methodology for adherence measures and describes the standard 80% threshold for many chronic medication classes.
PQA Adherence Measures
Star Ratings
Medication adherence matters economically because it is part of Medicare Part D Star Ratings.
CMS lists Part D medication adherence measures for diabetes medications, hypertension/RAS antagonists, and statins in Star Ratings reporting.
CMS 2025 MA and Part D Star Ratings
HRSN
Transportation, food access, housing, and financial strain are recognized health-related social needs.
CMS defines HRSN as individual social and economic needs that affect health and health care use, including food, housing, utilities, health care access, and transportation.
CMS SDOH and HRSN · Medicaid.gov HRSN
Architecture
Twilio can support HIPAA-eligible communication workflows, but only with the right products, BAA, and customer-side safeguards.
Public wording should say “planned HIPAA-conscious architecture,” not “HIPAA compliant,” until Orivo has production controls, BAAs, policies, and security review in place.
Twilio and HIPAA · Twilio Help Center
Medication data
RxNorm is a credible medication normalization foundation.
The National Library of Medicine states RxNorm provides normalized names for clinical drugs and links to vocabularies used in pharmacy management and drug interaction software.
NLM RxNorm · NLM RxNorm Overview
Language control

Claims to soften or avoid.

Risky wordingWhy riskySafer replacement
“Orivo reduces ADEs.”No pilot or clinical outcomes yet.“Orivo is designed to capture patient-reported medication concerns and route potential safety signals to a pharmacist or care team for review.”
“Orivo improves PDC scores.”No measured pilot data yet.“Orivo is designed to support adherence workflows tied to PDC-tracked medication classes.”
“HIPAA compliant.”Compliance requires implemented controls, BAAs, policies, and operating procedures.“Designed for future HIPAA-aligned deployment with appropriate BAAs, audit logs, encryption, access controls, and security review.”
“Any country / any insurance.”Too broad operationally and regulatory-wise.“Designed to begin with U.S. SMS-first pilots and expand to multilingual workflows as integrations mature.”
“Only longitudinal conversational health dataset in existence.”Absolute and easy to challenge.“Long-term opportunity: build a longitudinal dataset of patient-reported medication concerns and access barriers.”
“Every other health app requires a smartphone.”Competitively overbroad.“Many digital health tools still assume apps, portals, downloads, or high digital literacy.”
