What Orivo is building

A communication layer between patient language and clinical action.

The first version should stay narrow: SMS intake, medication literacy support, barrier detection, care-team summarization, and safe escalation. That is more credible than claiming to solve every healthcare outcome on day one.

Patient language intake

Patients can describe symptoms, confusion, affordability problems, transportation barriers, or adherence issues in plain language.

Clinical summarization

The system converts messy patient language into concise, reviewable context for pharmacists, physicians, and care coordinators.

Human-in-the-loop routing

Urgent, uncertain, medication-related, or low-confidence situations are routed to the right human instead of being answered as if the AI knows.

Legal and credibility cleanup

What this site should not claim yet.

Until pilots, contracts, BAAs, security reviews, and clinical oversight are real, keep public claims careful. Strong positioning is good. Unsupported medical, performance, security, or compliance claims are not.

Avoid hard outcomes

Do not claim patient satisfaction, adherence lift, reduced ADEs, or faster care navigation unless backed by actual data.

Avoid compliance overclaiming

Say HIPAA-aligned design target or HIPAA-conscious architecture until legal review, BAAs, operational controls, and vendor agreements are in place.

Avoid medical advice

Use clear language that Orivo supports education and routing. It does not diagnose, prescribe, or tell patients to stop medications.
